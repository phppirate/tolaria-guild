# The Case for Paul as Architect of a New Religion

### 1. The epistemic foundation is unfalsifiable by design

Paul never met Jesus. His entire claim to authority rests on a private revelation no one witnessed (Gal 1:11–12). He explicitly boasts that he did *not* consult "flesh and blood" — including the people who actually knew Jesus — before launching his mission (Gal 1:16–17). This is not a disciple preserving a tradition; it is a man declaring independence from the tradition's custodians. His knowledge of Jesus's earthly life is, by his own account, essentially zero — he never quotes a single teaching of Jesus in his letters except the eucharist formula and one contested divorce saying. The founder of "Christianity" as we know it shows almost no interest in the historical Christ.

### 2. The theological content is a different religion

Set the Synoptic Jesus and Paul side by side:

**The Synoptic Jesus:**

- Proclaims the *Kingdom of God* — Jesus is the messenger, not the message
- Salvation through repentance, forgiveness, and *doing* the will of the Father (Matt 7:21, 25:31–46 — judgment by works, explicitly)
- "Why do you call me good? No one is good but God alone" (Mark 10:18)
- Upholds the Law: "not one jot or tittle shall pass" (Matt 5:18)
- Mission to Israel: "I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matt 15:24); Gentiles are "dogs" (Mark 7:27)
- When asked how to gain eternal life: keep the commandments (Mark 10:17–19) — no mention of believing in his death or resurrection

**Paul:**

- Proclaims *Christ himself* — the death and resurrection are the entire message (1 Cor 2:2)
- Salvation by faith alone, apart from works (Rom 3:28, Gal 2:16) — a direct inversion of Matthew 25's sheep-and-goats judgment
- Christ is divine, pre-existent, cosmic (Phil 2:6–11, Col 1:15–17)
- The Law is a curse, a prison guard, abolished (Gal 3:13, 3:24–25; Rom 10:4)
- The Gentile mission is the whole point — Israel's rejection is a theological feature (Rom 9–11)
- Eternal life through believing in the resurrection (Rom 10:9)

A first-century Jew hearing both would not recognize these as the same teaching. Jesus preaches Jewish covenantal ethics with apocalyptic urgency; Paul preaches a Hellenistic salvation cult with a crucified-and-risen savior at its center — a category with ready parallels in the mystery religions of his native Tarsus region.

### 3. He fought the eyewitnesses — and outlasted them

Galatians records open warfare: he curses anyone preaching a different gospel (1:8–9), dismisses the Jerusalem "pillars" as irrelevant (2:6), calls Peter a hypocrite to his face (2:11–14), and describes "false brothers" spying on his churches — almost certainly Jerusalem emissaries (2:4). The Jerusalem faction's own position survives only in hostile descriptions: the Ebionites reportedly held that Paul was an apostate, that Jesus was a normal human prophet, and that the Law stood forever. That is plausibly *closer to what Jesus's own brother and original disciples believed* — and it was annihilated, first by Rome in 70 AD, then by the Pauline church's heresy machinery.

### 4. The historical accident that crowned him

The destruction of Jerusalem eliminated the only institutional check on his narrative. What survived the catastrophe was Paul's network: his letters, his churches, his traveling companions (Luke), his theological framework — into which the Gospels were then written. Mark (~70) already shows Pauline fingerprints; Luke-Acts retroactively domesticates Paul into a loyal subordinate of a Jerusalem church that no longer existed to object. The New Testament canon is not a neutral archive — it is the library of the winning faction.

### 5. The character evidence

By his own pen: a self-described shapeshifter ("all things to all people," 1 Cor 9:22), a man who admits to deceit-adjacent tactics ("being crafty, I caught you by trickery," 2 Cor 12:16 — his own defense against the accusation!), who protests his honesty with suspicious frequency, who built a theology in which *he personally* is the appointed apostle to the Gentiles, the interpreter of the mystery hidden for ages (Col 1:25–27, Eph 3:2–5). Every road in his system leads back to Paul's unique authority.

## The verdict this case supports

Paul took a Jewish apocalyptic preacher executed by Rome, whose actual message — repentance, the Kingdom, the Law, care for the poor — he barely mentions, and re-engineered him into the cosmic savior of a Gentile mystery cult: divine, pre-existent, salvific in death, accessible by faith alone, Law-free. He fought the people who knew Jesus, lost them to history's greatest convenient catastrophe, and his version became "Christianity."

Whether he did this cynically or sincerely, the engineering is the same. The religion of Jesus became the religion *about* Jesus — and Paul was the transformer.
